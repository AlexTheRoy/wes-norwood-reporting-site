Every populated Texas ZIP code scored for hail and hurricane, with strong wind and inland (river) flooding as smaller inputs, from the FEMA National Risk Index (December 2025 release) at census-tract resolution, weighted to where each ZIP's residents live, and grouped into four tiers. Hail is the peril most Texas homes share, from Dallas-Fort Worth and San Antonio to the Panhandle; hurricane is what sets the Gulf Coast, the Golden Triangle, the Coastal Bend and the Rio Grande Valley apart. Section 04 is the recommended treatment for Home leads, section 05 covers Dallas-Fort Worth, where about a third of your leads come from, then Bexar County (San Antonio), your second-largest lead county, with Houston and El Paso in brief, and section 06 matches the tiers against the 3,992 GOAL leads delivered from February 10 to October 19, 2026, about eight months of leads across all four campaigns (2,726 Home and 1,266 Auto). 03

Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution

What goes into the score
  • HailNRI hail risk: how often damaging hail falls times the building value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally. Half of Texas housing sits in ZIPs scoring above 88.1, so the cut points are set within Texas.
  • HurricaneNRI hurricane risk: modeled hurricane wind frequency and strength times the value exposed. Florida and the Carolinas set the top of the national scale, so Texas coastal ZIPs score in the low to mid 90s; the cut points are set for Texas.
  • Supporting inputsNRI strong wind and inland flooding risk: added to the composite score at lower weight. Strong wind matters across North and East Texas, where it lifts some mid-90s hail ZIPs into the High tier on the combined score.
  • Coastal floodingNRI coastal flooding risk is not in the composite, but a score of 90+ lifts a shoreline ZIP to at least High, so storm-surge ZIPs that FEMA's hurricane wind model scores lower are not left in Low. Tornado is in the spreadsheet only. Flood, coastal or inland, is excluded from a standard home policy and is quoted separately.
  • AvailabilityCarrier models (roof age and material, construction, elevation, distance to the coast) are property-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same hazards.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeHail score 98+ or hurricane score 94+
  • HighHail score 97+, hurricane score 91+, composite score 75+, or coastal flooding score 90+ (a floor for shoreline ZIPs such as Galveston Island)
  • ModerateHail score 94+, hurricane score 88+, or composite score 70+
  • LowEverything else.
  • How Extreme is setExtreme is sized to mark about one Texas home in nine (11.5% of housing). The hail arm (98+) covers 6.4% of Texas housing and the hurricane arm (94+) covers 5.1%. High and Moderate widen each arm, and the composite catches ZIPs where several perils add up.

04

Home leads: recommended treatment

Extreme tier gets an appetite check · High tier gets a roof, wind and deductible review

Check appetite · Extreme · 396 ZIPs · 11.5% of TX housing Roof, wind and deductible review · High · 463 ZIPs · 15.8% No change · 1064 ZIPs · 72.7%
Excluding every Extreme ZIP would remove 11.5% of Texas housing from Home; adding the High tier would bring it to 27.3%. We do not recommend a blanket exclusion. We recommend excluding only the ZIPs where new home business cannot be written today, and treating the rest of the Extreme and High tiers as a conversation: roof age and material, how the wind and hail deductible works, and windstorm and flood questions near the coast.
Home treatment by tier
  • ExtremeCheck appetite. Confirm Allstate is writing new home business in the ZIP before the first call. In hail ZIPs, ask the roof's age and material and whether it is impact-resistant, and explain the wind and hail deductible. In coastal hurricane ZIPs, ask how wind is written today, whether the home has storm shutters or roof tie-downs, and offer a flood quote.
  • HighRoof, wind and deductible review. Ask the year the roof was replaced and whether it is impact-resistant, and walk through the wind and hail deductible, often a percentage of the dwelling amount, before the first storm season.
  • ModerateNo change. Standard quote; roof age is still worth asking anywhere hail is common.
  • LowNo change. Standard quote.
Two kinds of Extreme ZIP
  • Hail242 ZIPs reach Extreme on hail (score 98+), holding 781,554 housing units: West Texas & Panhandle 177, Dallas-Fort Worth 35, San Antonio & South Texas 19, East Texas 8, Austin & Central Texas 3.
  • Hurricane157 ZIPs reach Extreme on hurricane (score 94+), holding 612,888 housing units: San Antonio & South Texas 61, Houston & Gulf Coast 52, Rio Grande Valley 27, Golden Triangle 12, East Texas 5.
  • Both3 ZIPs meet both rules.

Hail and hurricane in the conversation

Questions that make a Texas home quote faster and stickier

Why it matters here
  • Roofs40% of housing in Dallas-Fort Worth, San Antonio & South Texas, Austin & Central Texas and West Texas sits in ZIPs scoring 95+ for hail nationally. Roof age, material and whether the roof is impact-resistant belong in the first conversation, not the renewal.
  • DeductiblesWind and hail deductibles are often a percentage of the dwelling amount. Explaining the dollar figure on the first call avoids a surprise after the first storm and builds trust.
  • Hurricane54% of housing in the four coastal regions sits in ZIPs scoring 88+ for hurricane. Near the coast, ask how wind is written today, about shutters and roof tie-downs, and the age of the roof covering.
  • Flooding24.4% of Texans live in tracts FEMA rates high for inland flooding and 0.4% for coastal flooding. Flood is excluded from a standard home policy, so a flood quote is a natural follow-up in Houston and along the coast.
  • AutoHail and flood damage to a car is paid under comprehensive coverage. On every Auto quote in a High or Extreme ZIP, confirm comprehensive is included; it is also the natural opening to quote the home.

Data & files

  • Risk sourceFEMA National Risk Index, census tract table, December 2025 release (hail, hurricane, strong wind, inland flooding, coastal flooding, tornado), all 6,883 Texas tracts. The December 2025 release reports river flooding as inland flooding.
  • GeographyU.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and the 2020 ZCTA-to-tract relationship file. The file reports land-area overlaps, not population, so each tract's values are weighted into a ZIP by the tract's population times the share of its land inside the ZIP. Tract parts outside Texas are not scored. Housing counts from ACS 2020–2024.
  • LeadsAll 3,992 GOAL leads for this account across all four campaigns (two Home, two Auto), Feb 10 to Oct 19, 2026, about eight months of leads (2,726 Home, 1,266 Auto), counted by the ZIP on the lead. 2,720 of 2,726 Home leads and 1,258 of 1,266 Auto leads matched a populated Texas ZIP. 6 Home and 8 Auto leads came from PO-box or non-residential ZIPs (75267, 76195, 77225, 77243, 77250, 77413, 78074, 78288, 78627, 78646, 79409, 79926, 95747) with no Census profile.
  • LimitsA ZIP score describes the ZIP, not a house. Roof age and material, construction, elevation and distance to the water decide each quote; the tiers decide where to ask first.
  • FilesWes-Norwood-TX-ZIP-Data-10-19-26.csv · every ZIP, every score, tier and treatment column, plus the demographics used in the companion report.
